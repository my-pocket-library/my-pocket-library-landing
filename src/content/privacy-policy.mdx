My Pocket Library is a personal book library app for scanning ISBN barcodes, organizing books, tracking reading status, adding notes, managing shelf locations, sending direct messages to friends, and optionally sharing limited profile and book information with other readers.

This policy explains what we collect, why, how long we keep it, and what you can ask us to do with it. If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, it also serves as our notice under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Camera access is used to scan ISBN barcodes and book spines, to photograph a book's cover when you choose to, and to take a profile or book club photo. Scanning happens entirely on your device — scan images are never uploaded to us or to anyone else, and we never store them. A cover photo you take is stored with your own copy of the book, visible only to you. A profile or book club photo is uploaded, because other readers see it.

Before your name, username, bio, shelf caption, a book club's name, a message or a book title you type in is posted, the app checks it on your device against a list of objectionable words; nothing is sent anywhere for that check. A name supplied by Sign in with Apple or Google goes through the same check. When you set a new profile photo or book club picture, our server sends it to **Google Cloud Vision SafeSearch** in the EU, which rates how likely it is to be sexual or violent, and a photo it flags is removed. Google uses the image only to return that rating. Screening is limited to twenty photos per reader a day; a photo set past that limit is removed without being screened.

## Books you add to the catalogue

When no book database knows an ISBN, you can add the book yourself. The details you type are sent to us so we can add the book to the shared catalogue. If you leave "Credit me" on, and we add the book, its page shows every reader that you added it (by your username), and the cover photo on your copy may become the book's cover for everyone. If you turn it off, we never show your name on the book and never publish your photo. Deleting your account removes your name from every book you added.

We do not sell your personal information, we do not share it with advertisers, and we do not use it to build profiles or make automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you.

## Activity sharing

Activity updates (books added, started and finished) are public by default, including for existing accounts: anyone signed in to Pocket Library can see them. Choose Friends only in Settings → Privacy → Activity sharing, or from the Activity feed, to restrict existing and future updates to your accepted friends. Your choice syncs across devices. Blocking in either direction prevents access to activities. Books already hidden from your profile do not generate new activity updates. Borrow requests remain friend-only. Likes follow the same audience as the activity: anyone who can see an activity can like it, and its owner is notified with the display name of the reader who liked it. Readers who can see an activity can see its like count, but not a list of who liked it.

## Direct messages

Direct messages between you and your friends are stored on our Firebase servers. They are **not end-to-end encrypted**, which means that, technically, they are readable by our infrastructure. We want to be plain about what that does and does not mean:

- **We do not read your messages.** Nobody at My Pocket Library browses, searches, mines, or analyses message content, and we never use it to train anything, target anything, or share it with a third party.
- Our security rules make your messages unreadable to any other user of the app. Only the two participants in a conversation can load it, and if one of them blocks the other, the blocked reader can no longer load it either, except for the messages they sent themselves.
- The only automated processing of message content is the notification banner: when you receive a message, a server function reads the newest message solely to build the preview text sent to your device. All it writes back are two timestamps on that same message, recording that the message has already been counted into your unread badge and already announced, so that a repeated delivery cannot count it twice. Those marks are part of the message: they are deleted with it and included with it in your data export. Nothing is retained beyond the message itself.
- We access message content **only** where we are legally compelled to, or where it is strictly necessary to investigate a specific report of abuse or a specific bug you have reported to us. Any such access is deliberate, exceptional, and limited to the conversation in question.
- When you report a conversation or a book club message, the report carries a copy of the reported reader's last sixteen messages there. That copy is what we act on, so investigating a report does not mean reading the rest of the conversation. If those messages break our Terms, we delete them — only the ones the reported reader sent.
- **Opening a conversation is visible to the friend you are talking to.** We store one timestamp per conversation recording when you last opened it, which is what marks your unread badge cleared and what shows the other participant "Seen" under their last message. It is a single time per conversation, not a record of each message you read, and only the two of you can see it. There is currently no way to turn it off; if that matters to you, it is worth knowing before you open a message.
- Administrative access to our database is restricted to accounts that require it, protected by two-factor authentication.

If you need a guarantee that no operator can technically read your messages, use a dedicated end-to-end encrypted messenger. We would rather say this clearly than imply a protection we do not provide.

## Where your data is stored

Your library, profile, friend data and messages are stored in Google Cloud Firestore in a **European multi-region** (Belgium and the Netherlands), the server functions that send notifications run in Belgium, and photo screening uses Google Cloud Vision's EU endpoint. Your core app data therefore stays in the EU.

Some of our providers process limited data outside the EEA, principally in the United States:

- **Firebase Authentication** (Google) processes sign-in identifiers and email addresses.
- **Firebase Cloud Messaging** (Google) processes device tokens and notification payloads.
- **RevenueCat** processes subscription status and an account identifier.
- **Google Sign-In** and **Sign in with Apple** process your sign-in when you choose them.

Where personal data is transferred outside the EEA or the UK, it is covered by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses under the data processing terms we have accepted with each provider.

## Who else processes your data

We use the following providers as data processors, each under data processing terms that require them to protect your data to the same standard as this policy:

- **Google (Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Messaging, Cloud Functions, Installations, Cloud Vision)** — authentication, data storage, notifications, and screening new profile and book club photos.
- **Google Sign-In** — when you choose to sign in with Google.
- **Apple** — Sign in with Apple, and App Store purchase processing.
- **RevenueCat** — subscription entitlement management.

We also query **Open Library** and **ISBNdb** to look up book metadata from ISBNs, and load book covers from their servers. These requests carry the ISBN or search term, and, like any request over the internet, your device's network address. They do not carry your account identifier, your name, or anything else that identifies you.

Payment card details are handled entirely by Apple. We never see or store them.

## How long we keep your data

- **Account, library and profile data** is kept for as long as your account exists.
- **Direct messages** are kept until the conversation is deleted, which happens when either participant removes the friendship or deletes their account. Blocking is the exception: it ends the friendship but keeps the conversation for the reader who blocked, and the reader who was blocked keeps access only to the messages they sent. Deleting a conversation from your own list is not the same thing: it hides that conversation from you, and the messages stay until one of you ends the friendship or deletes their account, because the other reader's copy is not yours to delete. A conversation you have hidden is still included in your data export.
- **A block is visible to the reader you block.** They are told you blocked them when they open your profile or your conversation. They cannot see a list of who has blocked them. A block also hides their messages from you in book clubs you share, and stops book club invitations between you.
- **Your friend list is visible to your friends.** A reader you have accepted can see who else you have connected with, by name and profile picture, and can open those readers' profiles. Readers who are not your friends cannot see your friend list at all. Removing a friendship or blocking a reader takes each of you off the other's list.
- **Activity likes** are kept until you remove the like, the friendship ends, either reader blocks the other, the activity is deleted, or either account is deleted. Anyone who can see the activity can like it, and sees its like count and their own like state; the activity's owner is notified with the display name of each reader who likes it. No list of likers is exposed.
- **Book-borrow requests** are visible only to the two readers and contain public book and identity snapshots, not private shelf locations, notes or reading progress. Pending requests become unavailable or are removed when the copy is hidden, deleted or lent, or when the friendship ends or either reader blocks the other. Resolved requests remain in the inbox while the friendship and exposed book remain, and are removed when either account is deleted.
- **Shared book loans** remain visible to the lender and borrower after an unfriend or block so either person can record the physical book's return. Returned loans remain in both readers' histories until either account is deleted, at which point the shared record is removed.
- **Push tokens** are kept while notifications are allowed for the app. Turning them off in iOS Settings, signing out or deleting your account removes this device's token.
- **Abuse reports** are kept for as long as we need them to act on the report and to recognise repeat behaviour, and no longer than two years.
- **Moderation fingerprints** of profile text we removed are deleted with your account; those of removed photos are kept for up to two years. A fingerprint is a hash, not the text or the photo.
- **Support requests and catalog feedback** are kept so you can read our reply under Support → Your requests, and no longer than two years after you last sent them. They are removed when you delete your account, and you can ask us to delete any one of them.
- **Subscription records** at RevenueCat are kept while your account exists and deleted with it. Apple keeps its own record of your purchases.
- **Deleted data** is removed from our live database immediately. Firestore retains a one-hour version history for disaster recovery, after which deleted data is unrecoverable.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

- **Access** the personal data we hold about you, and receive a copy of it.
- **Rectify** data that is inaccurate or incomplete — most of it you can edit directly in the app.
- **Erase** your data. You can delete your account in the app at any time: tap your avatar, then Account. This removes your profile, library, notes, locations, wishlist, reading activity and activity likes, book-borrow requests, shared book loans, support requests and catalog feedback, friend records, book club memberships, push tokens, username reservation and every conversation you are part of — except that in a conversation someone has blocked you from, it removes the messages you sent and leaves theirs — and it deletes your sign-in, revokes Pocket Library's access to your Google account if you signed in with Google, and deletes your subscription record at RevenueCat. Abuse reports are the exception: a report you filed or one about you is kept for the period above, so we can recognise repeat behaviour.
- **Restrict** or **object to** our processing where we rely on legitimate interests.
- **Port** your data. The Account screen has an "Export my data" option that produces a JSON file containing your profile, library, notes, locations, wishlist, reading activity and activity likes, book-borrow requests, shared book loans, support requests and catalog feedback, friends, book clubs and conversations (only your own messages in a conversation someone has blocked you from), which you can save or send anywhere you like.
- **Withdraw consent** at any time where we rely on it, such as notifications, without affecting processing carried out before you withdrew it.
- **Complain** to your local data protection supervisory authority. In the EU you can find yours at edpb.europa.eu; in the UK it is the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Account deletion and data export are both available in the app, on the Account screen. A suspended account cannot sign in to use either, so if yours is suspended, email us and we will delete or export your data for you. For access, rectification, restriction or objection requests, email us at the address above and we will respond within one month.

One limitation we want to be transparent about: if you sent a book club invitation that the recipient has not yet answered, your display name may remain in their pending invitations after you delete your account, until that invitation is acted on. Email us and we will remove it.

No method of electronic storage or transmission is completely secure, and we will notify affected users and the relevant supervisory authority of a personal data breach where the GDPR requires it.
